How it was built

Today's home visits

CareOps flags the visits likely to go wrong, reads family messages in English and Hinglish, and proposes a fix for a coordinator to approve.

About the risk model and its known limitation Risk tiers are capacity-aware, not a fixed probability cutoff: top ~10% of today's visits by predicted disruption risk are flagged High, next ~10% Medium — 20% flagged in total, the same coordinator-bandwidth assumption used throughout this project's economics, fairness, and intervention-simulation modules — so the list stays reviewable in a coordinator's day rather than growing with the model's raw sensitivity. Predictions and reasons are model output (logistic regression on 6 features); recommended actions are rule-based, not yet AI-generated. This is one representative simulated day, not a cherry-picked best case — tier accuracy is directionally reliable in aggregate but noisy on any single day given current model strength.

Known limitation: first-time-patient predictions are overconfident relative to observed disruption in this small subgroup — mean predicted risk 25.6% against an actual disruption rate of 17.0% (a +8.6-point calibration gap, vs. +1.3 points for returning patients), on a subgroup 66x smaller than the returning-patient one. This is a calibration issue, not a flagging-rate issue: the subgroup check in fairness_subgroup_analysis.py found first-time patients are actually flagged less often than returning patients (10.6% vs 20.1%) and have a lower false-positive rate (10.3% vs 18.0%). Calibration should be reviewed before any production use. All data is simulated for portfolio purposes.
Voice visit reports
A caregiver's voice note becomes a structured report and a family update. Deterministic checks after the model decide what is trusted: a vital must actually appear in the note, red flags force a coordinator review, and the family update is held until then.
