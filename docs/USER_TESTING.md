# Coordinator user testing kit

**Goal:** find out whether CareOps saves a real coordinator time and whether they trust its suggestions. Run 3 to 5 sessions of about 30 minutes each, on a video call with screen share, using the [live demo](https://careops-live-demo-18xj.onrender.com/).

**Who to recruit:** people who schedule or coordinate home-care, nursing or caregiver visits (care agencies, hospital home-care desks, elder-care startups). Ask on LinkedIn, in alumni groups, or through people you have worked with. Say up front that it is a 30-minute research call for a portfolio project, with no sales pitch.

**Consent:** ask before recording. Never ask for real patient names or details; the demo uses synthetic data only.

---

## 1. Warm-up (5 minutes)

- Walk me through yesterday. How many visits did you handle?
- When did a visit last go wrong? What happened, and what did you do, step by step?
- How long did fixing it take? What was the most annoying part?
- How do families usually message you? In what language?

## 2. Tasks (15 minutes)

Ask them to think aloud. Don't help unless they are stuck for more than a minute.

| # | Task | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Open the page. Which visits would you look at first, and why?" | Do they understand "Act first" and the reasons? |
| 2 | "Find the family who wants to reschedule. What would you do?" | Do they trust the proposed day? Would they send the drafted message as is? |
| 3 | "Open Running late. What would you do first?" | Is "Family not told yet" the right alarm? |
| 4 | "Open Fix a visit. A nurse has dropped out. Find a replacement." | Do the reasons match how they choose? Do they ask to fix all of one nurse's visits at once? |
| 5 | "Look at the visit reports. Would you send these updates to families?" | Do they agree with what was held for review? |

## Hypotheses from the persona walkthrough (confirm or reject)

- Disruptions arrive per nurse, not per visit, so a "nurse is out today" flow would save the most time.
- A ready-to-send Hinglish message is worth more than the risk score.
- "Escalated · unassigned" is clearer than any colour or icon.
- Coordinators want exact times and names; IDs are noise.
- Elder-companionship coordinators value the family update far more than risk ranking.

## 3. Wrap-up (10 minutes)

1. What would make you trust a suggestion enough to approve it?
2. What is missing that you would need on day one?
3. What would you remove?
4. If this existed, how much time would it save you per day? (Ask for a number.)
5. On a scale of 1 to 10, how likely are you to use it? Why not higher?
